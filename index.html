Rebuild in progress · Opening 2026

The robot works. Three weeks later nothing is left.

That is the normal outcome of build-based robotics classes, including the one we ran here until 2016. The child finishes a working project, the parent is pleased, and almost none of it survives the term. We are rebuilding ProgramBots around retention instead of activity.

01 / Why rebuild

A finished build is evidence of a good afternoon, not of learning.

Version one of ProgramBots taught builds. Kits, guided steps, a thing that moved at the end. It produced completion. It did not produce recall.

Nothing in that format asks a child to retrieve anything from memory under difficulty, and retrieval under difficulty is what makes knowledge stay. So the curriculum is being rewritten from zero against four decades of retention research, and the schedule is being built on the assumption that children forget, because they do.

The rebuild is a pedagogy change, not a coat of paint.

02 / The evidence we are building on
61% / 40% Recall of the same passage one week on. Self-testers against rereaders. The self-testers read it 3.4 times, the rereaders 14.2 times. Roediger & Karpicke, 2006
1σ Gain from mastery learning in a group setting. The average mastery student outperforms roughly 84% of conventionally taught students. No private tutor required. Bloom, 1984
2σ Gain from one-to-one tutoring plus mastery learning, the 98th percentile result. Unaffordable at scale, and the figure is contested. See section 05. Bloom, 1984

The 1 sigma number is the one that matters commercially. It is deliverable in a small group, at a price a parent will pay, by instructors who can be trained. That is the target ProgramBots is designed around.

03 / The method

Five principles, and what each one changes in the room.

P.01

Retrieval practice

Roediger & Karpicke, 2006

Every session opens cold. The child rebuilds last week's circuit or rewrites last week's function from memory before any new material is touched. No notes, no reference sheet.

P.02

Spaced repetition

Ebbinghaus, 1885 · Cepeda et al., 2006

Concepts return on a widening schedule: week 1, week 3, week 8, week 20. A loop learned in term one reappears inside a motor controller in term three.

P.03

Mastery gating

Bloom, 1984

No child advances on attendance. A level closes only when the child can rebuild its core artefact unaided, from a blank breadboard and an empty file.

P.04

Desirable difficulty

Bjork & Bjork, 1992

Kits arrive incomplete and instructions arrive partial, on purpose. The struggle is the mechanism, not a flaw in the lesson plan. Comfort in week one predicts nothing in week ten.

P.05

Constructionism

Papert, Mindstorms, 1980

Every unit ends in a public, shareable object the child can explain to someone else. Explaining it is the assessment. If they cannot teach it, they did not learn it.

04 / Curriculum spine

Four levels. Each one gated on a rebuild from memory.

Level Name Age Spine Gate: rebuild unaided
L1 Signals 8 to 10 Sequence, loops, conditionals. Current, voltage, LEDs, switches. A timed traffic light on breadboard, coded and wired from blank.
L2 Systems 10 to 12 Python functions and state. Sensors, analogue input, serial. A sensor that reads the room and acts on a threshold the child sets.
L3 Control 12 to 14 Feedback loops, proportional control, motor drivers, power budgets. A line follower that recovers from being pushed off course.
L4 Autonomy 14 to 16 Perception, decision logic, embedded constraints, version control. A robot that completes a course it has not seen before.
05 / Evidence quality

Which of these claims is actually solid.

Education marketing cites research badly. Here is our own honest grading of what the page above rests on.

  • Strong Retrieval practice and spacing. Replicated across hundreds of studies, many age groups and many subjects. We would stake the whole curriculum on these two, and we have.
  • Moderate Mastery learning in groups. The direction of the evidence is consistent, the size of the effect varies by subject and by how strictly the gate is enforced.
  • Contested Bloom's exact 2 sigma figure. Small samples, tightly controlled conditions, and it has not been reliably reproduced at that magnitude. We quote it for context, not as a promise.
  • Not a number Papert's constructionism. A design philosophy, not a measured effect. It shapes how our units are built and we will not pretend it comes with an effect size.
